Where candidates lose marks

  • Writing

    Reaching for impressive vocabulary and using it wrongly, when a clear argument in accurate English would score higher.

  • Speaking

    Short answers, long pauses and repeated words when Part 3 moves to abstract questions.

  • Reading

    Spending too long on the first passage and running out of time on the third.

  • Preparation

    Following general tips from videos that never look at the mistakes you personally make.

What you get

  • Marked writing

    You write Task 1 and Task 2 answers between lessons. I mark each one against the four criteria examiners use: Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

  • Speaking mocks

    A full three-part speaking test under exam conditions, then written feedback on fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation.

  • Reading and listening technique

    How to manage the clock, where the answers usually sit, and the question types that catch people out.

  • A weekly plan

    What to practise each week between now and your test date, so your study time goes where the marks are.
