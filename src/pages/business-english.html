What we work on

You choose the priorities. Most people start with one or two of these.

  • Emails and messages

    Writing that is clear, polite and short. Saying no, chasing a reply, giving bad news, and getting the tone right for a client or a director.

  • Meetings and calls

    Joining in at the right moment, disagreeing without sounding rude, and following fast speakers and unfamiliar accents.

  • Presentations

    A structure people can follow, natural linking phrases, and calm answers to questions you did not prepare for.

  • Interviews and pay

    Strong answers about your experience, questions to ask them, and the language of negotiating a salary.

  • Pronunciation and clarity

    The handful of sounds and stress patterns that make people ask you to repeat yourself.

I use this English at work, not only in lessons.

Alongside tutoring I work in commercial estimating and project management for a UK company. On an ordinary day I price work, write to clients and suppliers, chase late answers and explain delays.

So when we practise a difficult email or a tense call, I am not teaching from a coursebook. I am showing you how those conversations sound between British colleagues, including the polite phrases that mean something less polite.

How the lessons run

  1. Bring real work

    An email you need to send, slides for next week, a meeting you are nervous about. Remove anything confidential first.

  2. We work through it

    You speak and write; I listen, stop you at the important mistakes and show you the better version.

  3. You get the corrections

    After each lesson I send your mistakes, the fixes and the phrases worth keeping.

  4. You use it the same week

    The email gets sent. The meeting happens. Next lesson we look at how it went.
