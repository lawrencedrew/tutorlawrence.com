Your CV in English: words that sound stronger

Start every bullet point with a specific action verb, say what changed because of you, and cut "responsible for". That one habit makes a CV sound more confident than any template. Here are the verbs, and the rewrites.

The problem with "responsible for"

Most CVs I see from non-native speakers are full of "Responsible for…", "Participation in…" and "Duties included…". They describe the job description, not the person. A reader learns what you were supposed to do, but not what you actually did or how well.

Compare:

Weak: "Responsible for supplier contacts."

Strong: "Negotiated new terms with 12 key suppliers, reducing average delivery times from ten days to six."

Same job. The second version has a verb that shows action, a number, and a result.

The bullet-point formula

Action verb + what you did + result.

  • "Introduced a weekly stock report, cutting time spent on manual checks by half a day a week."
  • "Trained four new team members on the order system during a busy peak season."
  • "Redesigned the onboarding checklist, which the whole department now uses."

If you don't have a number, describe the outcome: "now used by the whole department", "adopted across three sites", "praised by the client in their annual review". Only use figures you could explain in an interview.

Action verbs by skill

Use British spelling for a UK employer: analysed, organised, prioritised. American CVs use "-yzed" and "-ized", and a UK reader will notice.

Before and after

Weak or translatedStronger
Responsible for the preparation of monthly reports.Prepared monthly sales reports for the senior management team.
Participation in the implementation of a new CRM.Helped roll out a new CRM to 40 users, and ran training for the sales team.
Realisation of projects for clients.Delivered six client projects on time and within budget.
Contact with customers.Handled around 30 customer queries a day by phone and email.
I was working on improving processes.Streamlined the invoice approval process, removing two manual steps.

Notice "realisation" and "implementation" in the left column. Nouns built from verbs are common in CVs in many European languages, but in English they sound heavy. Turn the noun back into a verb and the sentence comes alive.

Grammar rules for CV bullet points

  • No "I". Bullet points start with the verb: "Managed a team of five", not "I managed a team of five".
  • Past tense for past jobs, present tense for your current one: "Manage a team of five" (now) and "Managed a team of five" (before). Some people use past tense throughout for achievements, which is also fine. Be consistent within each job.
  • Don't use the continuous. "Was managing" or "Managing" sounds unfinished. Use "Managed".
  • Watch false friends. "Realise" in English means "understand", not "carry out". "Actual" means "real", not "current".

Words to cut

Some words appear on so many CVs that readers skip them:

  • Hard-working, motivated, dynamic, team player, results-driven. Everyone says them. Show these qualities through your bullet points instead.
  • Various, different, several. "Worked on various projects" says nothing. Name one, or give the number.
  • Helped with, assisted in, when you actually did the work. Use these only when someone else genuinely led it.

That last point matters. Many people undersell themselves out of modesty. If you ran the project, say "ran". British readers won't think it's boasting if it's true and specific.

Personal details on a UK CV

A UK CV is different from many European ones in what it leaves out. Official UK careers advice says you should not include your age, date of birth, marital status or nationality. Put your name, phone number, email and a link to your professional networking profile at the top. Photos are also not normally expected on a UK CV.

Source: National Careers Service, CV sections. Check it before you apply in case anything has changed.

If your right to work in the UK isn't obvious from your CV, the application form will usually ask about it separately.

Use the same verbs in the interview

The verbs that strengthen your CV also strengthen your spoken answers. "I streamlined the process" sounds more convincing than "I made some changes to the process".
