Word stress: why native speakers misunderstand you

If people ask you to repeat words you said clearly, the problem is probably stress, not your accent. Native speakers recognise words by their rhythm first, so the wrong syllable can make a familiar word unrecognisable.

Why stress matters more than sounds

Every English word with more than one syllable has one syllable that is stronger: a little louder, longer and higher than the others. In this guide I show it in capitals: deVELop, COLleague, inforMAtion.

Here is what a working teacher notices quickly. Students with strong accents are often perfectly easy to understand, while students with near-perfect sounds are sometimes hard to follow, because the stress is in the wrong place. A British listener hears "DE-ve-lop" and their brain searches for a word that starts with a strong "DE". It doesn't find one. They say "Sorry?", and you repeat it the same way, louder, which doesn't help.

Get the stress right and you can get a vowel wrong and still be understood. The reverse is much less true.

The other half: weak syllables

Stress isn't only about making one syllable strong. The unstressed syllables in English become short and relaxed, often just a quick "uh" sound. That contrast is what creates the rhythm.

  • photographer: fuh-TOG-ruh-fuh
  • company: KUM-puh-nee
  • available: uh-VAY-luh-bul
  • today: tuh-DAY

Many languages give every syllable a full, clear vowel. If you do that in English, even with the stress in the right place, you sound slow and over-careful. Relax the weak syllables and your speech becomes clearer, not less clear.

Where your first language pushes you

Some stress mistakes are predictable from your first language:

  • Hungarian stresses the first syllable of every word, so speakers tend to say DE-velop, EM-ployee, CON-tribute. The English versions are deVELop, emPLOYee (or employEE) and conTRIBute.
  • Polish usually stresses the second-to-last syllable, so long words like "development" or "economic" can drift to the wrong place.
  • Spanish and Italian speakers often carry over the stress of a similar word from their language: "inteRESting" instead of INteresting (from interesante or interessante), or "comforTAble" instead of COMfortable.

If you know your habit, you know which words to check. If you are a Hungarian speaker, also see common mistakes Hungarian speakers make in English.

Noun or verb? The stress changes

A group of two-syllable words change stress depending on their job. As a noun, the stress is usually on the first syllable. As a verb, it moves to the second.

NounVerbExample
a REcordto reCORDWe need to reCORD the meeting for the REcord.
a PREsentto preSENTI'll preSENT the figures on Monday.
an INcreaseto inCREASEWe expect an INcrease, so we'll inCREASE prices.
a CONtractto conTRACTThe CONtract is signed. (The verb usually means "get smaller" or "catch an illness".)
an OBjectto obJECTDoes anyone obJECT?
PROgressto proGRESSWe're making PROgress. Talks proGRESSED well.
an EXportto exPORTEXports rose, so we'll exPORT more next year.

Endings that tell you where the stress goes

You don't have to learn every word separately. Some endings are reliable guides.

  • -tion and -sion: stress the syllable just before. inforMAtion, deCIsion, presenTAtion, organiSAtion.
  • -ic and -ical: stress the syllable just before. ecoNOMic, straTEgic, poLItical, PRACtical.
  • -ity: stress the syllable just before. aBILity, responsiBIlity, priORity.
  • -ee and -eer: the ending itself is stressed. trainEE, engiNEER, voluntEER.

This explains why word families move: eCONomy, ecoNOMic, eCONomist. PHOtograph, phoTOGrapher, photoGRAPHic. When you learn a new form of a word, check the stress again. Don't assume it stays put.

Compound nouns: stress the first part

When two words form one thing, the first word usually gets the stress: MEETing room, DEADline, SPREADsheet, CAR park, BUSiness plan. Compare a "GREENhouse" (a glass building for plants) with a "green HOUSE" (a house that is green). In meetings, "SALES team" and "PROject manager" sound right; "sales TEAM" sounds like you are contrasting it with a different sales group.

Words I correct most often at work

  • COLleague, not colLEAGUE
  • COMfortable, not comFORtable
  • deVELop and deVELopment, not DEvelop
  • deTERmine, not DEtermine
  • CATegory, not caTEgory
  • ANalyse, but aNALysis
  • hoTEL (stress at the end, unusually)
  • eVENT, not EVent

Two quick ways to practise

  1. Hum the word. Close your mouth and hum the rhythm: "deVELop" is "mm-MM-mm". If your hum doesn't match the dictionary recording, your stress is off. It sounds silly, but it isolates the rhythm from the sounds.

  2. Clap or tap. Tap the table on every syllable, hard on the stressed one. Do this for the five longest words in your next presentation.

Every good online dictionary marks stress with a small vertical line before the stressed syllable, and has a British audio button. Use both whenever you learn a new word. For the sounds themselves, see words that are pronounced differently from how they look.
