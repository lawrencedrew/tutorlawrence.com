'I am agree' and other mistakes even advanced speakers make

Most advanced speakers don't have a grammar problem. They have about ten small habits that slipped in years ago and never left. Here are the ones I hear most, with the fix for each.

Why these mistakes survive

These mistakes rarely stop anyone understanding you. That is exactly why they last. Nobody corrects them in a meeting, so they get repeated hundreds of times and start to feel right. By C1 they are fossilised: you know the rule if I ask you, but the wrong version comes out when you speak quickly.

They do matter, though. A listener may not notice one, but five in a conversation make a strong speaker sound less polished than they are. In an interview or an exam, that costs you.

What is behind each group

Verbs that are not adjectives

"Agree" is a verb, so it doesn't need "am". The mistake comes from languages where "I am in agreement" is a single expression. The same thing happens with "I am depend" and "I am disagree".

One detail most textbooks skip: British speakers do say "We're agreed, then" or "Are we all agreed?" at the end of a meeting. Here "agreed" works like an adjective meaning "in agreement", and it is normally used about a group. So "We're agreed" is fine. "I am agree" is not. If in doubt, use "I agree".

Verbs that take a person directly, and verbs that don't

"Tell" takes the person straight after it: tell me, tell the client. "Say" and "explain" don't: say something to someone, explain something to someone. "Suggest" doesn't take "someone to do" either. Use "suggest that I call", "suggest calling" or "suggest I call".

"Discuss" goes the other way. It already contains the idea of "about", so the topic comes straight after it: discuss the budget. You can, of course, have a discussion about the budget, because that is a noun.

Fixed prepositions

"Depend on", "interested in", "responsible for", "good at", "married to". These don't follow logic, so learn them as one unit, not as a word plus a preposition. I ask students to say the pair aloud in a full sentence about their own job, three times. It sounds silly, and it works.

-ed and -ing

"Interested" describes how you feel. "Interesting" describes the thing that causes the feeling. "I'm interesting" means you are fascinating to other people. The same goes for bored and boring, confused and confusing, tired and tiring.

"To" that is a preposition, not part of a verb

In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun or an -ing form: I look forward to hearing from you, I look forward to the meeting. The same pattern appears in "I'm used to working late" and "When it comes to pricing…".

Uncountable nouns and group nouns

"Information", "advice" and "feedback" have no plural. "People" is already plural, so it takes "are". I cover the first group in detail in countable and uncountable nouns.

Tense with "since"

"Since 2021" connects the past to now, so English uses the present perfect: I've worked here since 2021. Many languages use the present tense here, which is why this one is so stubborn. There is a full guide on since and for.

"According to me"

"According to" introduces somebody else's view or a source: according to the report, according to our lawyer. For your own view, use "In my opinion", "I think" or, in a meeting, "From where I'm sitting…".

How to actually fix a fossilised mistake

Knowing the rule isn't enough, because you already know it. What works is changing the habit in your mouth.

  1. Pick two mistakes, not twelve. Choose the two you are sure you make. Working on all of them at once means fixing none.

  2. Write one sentence for each about your real work. "I agree with Marta about the launch date." "Could you explain the new process to me?"

  3. Say them aloud every morning for a week. Out loud, at normal speed. Silent reading doesn't change speech.

  4. Catch yourself, then repeat the full sentence. When the old version comes out in a call, say the correct version straight after. Self-correction is normal for native speakers too, and nobody will think twice.

  5. Record a two-minute voice note about your week at the end of the week, and listen for your two mistakes. When they've gone, pick the next two.
