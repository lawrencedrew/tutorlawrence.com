Common mistakes that cost points in the ECL exam

Many lost ECL points have nothing to do with your level of English. They come from the exam's own rules: answers that say too much, bullet points left out, and answers that never reach the answer sheet. Here is what to avoid in each module.

Why small mistakes matter more now

Since August 2026, you need at least 60% in each module to pass it. In reading and listening that is 12 of 20 items, each worth 1.25 points. Two or three careless answers can be the difference between passing a module and retaking it.

Source: ECL, Passing ECL exams and exam format page. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

Reading and listening

1. Writing too much in short answers

The regulations say short questions should be answered in a few words, not full sentences, and that an answer containing unnecessary information is marked wrong. That includes reading answers copied from the text with extra information.

Marked wrongMarked right
"The train, because the flights were cancelled and the roads were busy""The train"
"In March or possibly April"The month the speaker confirms

Hedging with two answers doesn't work either. If one of them is wrong, the answer contains something wrong.

2. Leaving answers in the booklet

Only answers on the official answer sheet are evaluated. Candidates who work in the question booklet and run out of time to copy across lose points they had already earned. Transfer as you go, or leave five minutes at the end.

3. Unclear corrections

Write in black or blue ink. Correction fluid and pens with erasers aren't allowed. To change an answer, cross it out and write the new one clearly. In answer grids, cross out the wrong letter and write your final answer in the same box. Only clearly marked answers are accepted.

Source: ECL exam regulations and FAQ. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

4. Choosing the answer with the matching word

In matching and multiple-choice items, the option that repeats a word from the text or recording is often the trap. The correct answer usually says the same thing in different words. Match ideas, not vocabulary. There is more on this in ECL C1 reading strategies.

5. Panicking in the first listening

Each recording is played twice. Stopping to agonise over one missed answer in the first listening means you miss the next two. Leave it, and catch it the second time.

Writing

6. Missing or rushing a bullet point

This is the most expensive writing mistake. The official criteria give the top task-completion score only when all four bullet points are covered and developed. If none is developed, the whole task scores 0, whatever the quality of the English.

The usual cause is time. Task 1 gets 45 minutes, and task 2 gets a rushed final paragraph. Plan both tasks at the start, or set a firm halfway point.

7. One long block of text

Paragraphing is part of the written accuracy criterion, and ECL's own writing guide reminds candidates to separate paragraphs. Use a line space between them so the assessor can see your structure at a glance.

Sources: ECL, Criteria for assessing writing and writing guide. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

8. Wrong register

Style is one of the five criteria. A formal email that opens "Hi guys" or a forum post that reads like a legal document both lose points. Before you write, decide who the reader is and how you would speak to them.

Wrong for a formal emailBetter
"I wanna know if you got any rooms left.""I would be grateful if you could tell me whether any rooms are still available."
"Write back ASAP.""I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience."

9. Using the dictionary to find impressive words

The dictionary is allowed in writing only, and it's best used to check spelling and collocations. A rare word picked from a list of translations is often slightly wrong in context, and the C1 vocabulary criterion rewards words that are well chosen, not just rare.

Speaking

10. Two monologues instead of a conversation

In the guided conversation you are talking with your partner. If each of you gives a long speech and waits, you show very little interaction. Keep turns short and ask your partner questions: "Would you agree?", "What's it like where you live?"

11. Describing the pictures one by one

"In picture one there is… in picture two there is…" uses up your time with simple language. Group the pictures, compare them, speculate and give an opinion. See how to pass the ECL C1 speaking exam for a structure.

12. Reciting prepared answers

There's no preparation time, and the bullet points rarely match what you memorised. Prepare vocabulary and opinions for each topic, not scripts.

On the day

  • Arrive early. You won't be admitted once the listening module has started.
  • Leave your phone and smartwatch outside. Electronic devices aren't allowed in the exam room.
  • Bring a printed dictionary for writing, not a thematic one, and no handwritten notes or word lists.
