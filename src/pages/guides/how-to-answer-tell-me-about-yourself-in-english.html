How to answer 'Tell me about yourself' in English

Give a 60 to 90-second answer in three parts: what you do now, what you've done that matters for this job, and why you're here today. That's it. Here's how to build it and say it naturally.

What the interviewer actually wants

"Tell me about yourself" isn't a request for your life story. It's a warm-up and a test of one thing: can you summarise yourself clearly, in English, without rambling? The interviewer also uses your answer to choose their next questions, so whatever you mention, expect to be asked about it.

That second point is the useful one. Your answer is a chance to steer the interview towards your strongest material.

The three-part structure: present, past, future

  1. Present (2–3 sentences). Your current role, what you're responsible for, and one thing you're good at.

  2. Past (2–3 sentences). The experience that led you here. Pick only what's relevant to this job. Skip the rest.

  3. Future (1–2 sentences). Why this role, why now. End on the job you're interviewing for, so the interviewer has an easy next question.

Some people prefer past, present, future, which tells the story in order. Either works. I usually teach "present first" because it puts your strongest, most current information at the start, when the interviewer is paying most attention.

A sample answer

Here's an answer for a logistics coordinator applying for a supply chain analyst role:

"I'm currently a logistics coordinator at a distribution company in Poznań, where I manage inbound shipments for around forty suppliers. A big part of my job is spotting delays before they become problems, and I've built a few reports in Excel and Power BI that the team now uses every day.

Before that, I spent two years in customer service for a freight company, which taught me how the whole chain looks from the customer's side.

What I enjoy most now is the data side of the job, and I'd like to make that my main focus. That's why this analyst role caught my attention."

That's about 100 words, which takes roughly 45–60 seconds to say at a calm pace. Notice there's no date of birth, no hobbies, no list of every job since university.

Phrases that sound natural

PartPhrases
PresentI'm currently… / At the moment I work as… / My main responsibility is… / A big part of my job is…
PastBefore that, I… / I started out in… / That's where I learnt to…
FutureWhat I'd like to do next is… / That's why this role caught my attention. / I'm looking for a role where I can…

Use contractions: "I'm", "I've", "I'd". They make you sound relaxed and fluent. "I am currently working as" is correct, but in speech it sounds read aloud.

The mistakes I correct most often

AvoidSay instead
My name is Anna and I am 34 years old.I'm a project manager in the energy sector. (They already know your name, and age isn't relevant.)
I work in a company that produces…I work for a company that makes…
I have 8 years of experience in this field.I've been working in procurement for eight years.
I am responsible of the budget.I'm responsible for the budget.
I am a very hard-working and motivated person.I've built a few reports the team now uses every day. (Show it, don't claim it.)

The last row matters most. Adjectives about yourself are easy to say and easy to forget. One concrete detail is worth five adjectives.

A tip most people miss: the hook

Put one slightly interesting detail in your answer that invites a follow-up question you'd love to answer. In the sample above, it's "I've built a few reports that the team now uses every day". Most interviewers will ask, "Tell me more about those reports." Now you're talking about your best achievement, and you've prepared it.

If you know the STAR method, prepare a STAR story for your hook. The two fit together perfectly.

How to practise it

  • Write it, then cut it. Write your answer in full, then remove a third. First drafts are always too long.
  • Don't memorise it word for word. Memorise the three parts and two or three key phrases. A word-perfect script sounds flat, and if you lose one word, you lose the whole thing.
  • Record yourself. Time it on your phone. Over 90 seconds? Cut again.
  • Prepare a short version. Sometimes it comes as "Briefly, tell me about your background." Have a 30-second version ready too.
