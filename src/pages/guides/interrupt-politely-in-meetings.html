Phrases to get into a meeting when nobody pauses

"I never know when to speak in meetings" is something I hear every week. The problem isn't your English. It's that you're waiting for a pause that never comes.

Why waiting doesn't work

In English-speaking meetings, especially British ones, people rarely leave a clean silence between speakers. The next person starts while the last one is finishing. If you wait for a gap, the topic has usually moved on by the time you find one.

Native speakers get in with a short phrase that asks for the floor. It's polite, it's expected, and it tells everyone you're about to speak.

Five phrases that work in any meeting

  1. "Can I just come in here?" The most common one in British offices. "Just" makes it soft.
  2. "Sorry to jump in, but…" Good when someone is mid-flow and you need to add something now.
  3. "Building on what Anna said…" Connects your point to the last speaker, so it never sounds like an interruption.
  4. "Before we move on, could I add one thing?" Useful when the chair is about to close a topic.
  5. "Just to pick up on that point…" Takes the conversation back to something you want to discuss.

How to use them

Say the phrase, then pause for half a second. People stop talking and turn to you. That pause is your moment, and it feels much less awkward than talking over someone.

Keep your first sentence short. Once you have the floor, you can slow down.

On video calls, unmute first and use the person's name: "Sorry, Marek, can I just come in here?" Names cut through better than anything when audio overlaps.

What to avoid

  • "I want to say something." Grammatically fine, but it sounds abrupt in English.
  • "Excuse me, excuse me…" Repeating it sounds stressed. Say it once, or use one of the phrases above.
  • Raising your hand on a video call and waiting. In many teams nobody checks. Use your voice too.

Start with one

Don't try to learn all five at once. Pick the one that feels most natural, use it in your next meeting, and add another the week after. Phrases only become yours when you've said them out loud a few times.
