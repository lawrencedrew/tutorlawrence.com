Is the ECL exam accepted for university and work?

In Hungary, yes: ECL English is state-accredited from A2 to C1 and recognised by universities and employers. Outside Hungary it depends entirely on who is asking, so check before you book. Here is what the official sources say.

What ECL is

ECL is a European language exam system set up by a consortium of EU member states in 1992. The exams are aligned with the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) and are offered at A2, B1, B2 and C1 in 15 languages, including English. The examination centre is based in Pécs, Hungary.

Source: ECL, The ECL consortium. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

In Hungary

ECL exams in English, Hungarian and German at A2, B1, B2 and C1 are state-accredited in Hungary. According to the ECL Examination Centre, they are recognised by all universities and employers as proof of language proficiency. ECL certificates in other languages can be recognised through a nostrification process by the Hungarian education authority.

So if your Hungarian university or employer asks for a state-accredited B2 or C1 English certificate, an ECL certificate at that level qualifies. What you should still check is which level they need and whether they want a complex certificate covering all four skills, or accept a partial one.

Source: ECL, Recognition in Hungary. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

Outside Hungary

ECL's own labour market page gives examples from Germany, where several recognition and immigration authorities accept ECL certificates. These include offices dealing with nursing recognition and with skilled-worker immigration. Most of these examples concern German-language certificates, which matters if you are hoping to use an English one.

For other countries and for English certificates, the honest answer is: it depends on the receiving organisation. Many universities and employers abroad publish a list of accepted English tests. If ECL is on it, good. If it isn't, ask them directly in writing before you book.

Source: ECL, Labour market. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

What ECL can't be used for

If you need to prove your English for a UK visa, you need a Secure English Language Test from an approved provider on the UK government's list, such as IELTS for UKVI. ECL is not on that list. The same applies to many visa systems elsewhere, which name specific tests.

Source: GOV.UK, Prove your English language abilities with a secure English language test. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

If you're weighing up the options, see ECL vs IELTS vs Cambridge.

How long an ECL certificate lasts

ECL certificates have no expiry date. However, the organisation reading your certificate can set its own conditions, and some ask for a recent result. A digital PDF certificate is available on request through your exam site and has the same official value as the paper one when used electronically.

Source: ECL FAQ. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

The August 2026 change: why it matters for recognition

From August 2026, ECL became modular. You need at least 60% in a module to pass it, each passed module gets its own certificate, and if you pass all four modules within a year of your first exam you can request a complex certificate.

Two consequences for recognition:

  • A module certificate isn't a complex certificate. If an employer asks for a "complex" B2 or C1, you need all four modules.
  • Older certificates may show a skill below 60%. Under the pre-August 2026 rules you could pass with 40% in a skill if your average was 60%. Those certificates remain valid, but a university, employer or authority may set its own minimum per skill, so check its current requirement. If yours has a skill below 60%, check with the organisation, and if in doubt, consider retaking that module under the new system.

Sources: ECL, Passing ECL exams and FAQ. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

A short email to send before you book

If you're not sure, ask. Here is a template you can adapt:

"Dear Admissions Team,
I am planning to take the ECL English exam at C1 level. Could you confirm whether an ECL certificate is accepted as proof of English for [programme/position]? If so, do you require a complex certificate covering all four skills, and is there a minimum score for each skill?
Thank you for your help.
Kind regards,
[Name]"

A written answer protects you. A verbal "should be fine" from a receptionist does not.
