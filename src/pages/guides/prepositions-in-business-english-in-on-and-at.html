Prepositions in business English: in, on and at

There is a simple pattern for time and place, and then a set of fixed work phrases that just have to be learned. This guide gives you both, plus the British versions that differ from American English.

Time: from big to small

Think of it as a funnel. In is for longer periods, on for days, at for exact times.

PrepositionUsed withExamples
inmonths, years, seasons, quarters, parts of the dayin March, in 2027, in the summer, in Q3, in the morning
ondays and dateson Monday, on 4 June, on Friday afternoon, on my birthday
atclock times and short pointsat 3 pm, at noon, at the end of the day, at night

Notice the switch: in the morning, but on Monday morning. As soon as a day is named, it becomes "on".

And no preposition at all before next, last, this, every and tomorrow: "We'll meet next Tuesday", not "on next Tuesday".

The weekend

British English says at the weekend. American English says on the weekend. Both are understood everywhere, but if you work with British colleagues, "at the weekend" will sound more natural to them.

Deadlines: by, until and within

These cause more real problems at work than in, on and at, because they change what you are promising.

  • by Friday = no later than Friday. It could be Wednesday. Please send the figures by Friday.
  • until Friday = continuing up to Friday, then stopping. I'm on leave until Friday.
  • within five days = no more than five days from now. Invoices must be paid within 30 days.

"Send it until Friday" is a very common mistake and it doesn't make sense in English. If you mean a deadline, it is always "by". For more on this, see how to ask for something by a deadline.

Place and situation at work

At work, in the office, on a call

  • at work, at home, at the office (the location in general)
  • in the office (physically inside, not working from home): I'm in the office on Tuesdays and Thursdays.
  • in a meeting (busy, taking part): Sorry, she's in a meeting until 11.
  • at a meeting / at a conference (attending an event): I met him at the conference in Berlin.
  • on a call, on the phone, on Teams, on Zoom

Documents and screens

This is where I correct people most. The pattern: a surface takes "on", a container takes "in".

  • in the email, in the report, in the contract, in the attachment, in the spreadsheet, in the presentation
  • on the website, on page 4, on slide 6, on the screen, on the agenda, on the list, on the invoice

"On the invoice" works because the invoice is a single sheet you look at. Native speakers don't think about this; it's simply what sounds right. Learn the common ones as fixed phrases.

Fixed phrases worth knowing

Common mistakeNatural British English

At the end we chose the cheaper option.In the end we chose the cheaper option.

On time and in time

  • on time = at the planned time, punctual. The meeting started on time.
  • in time = early enough, before it's too late. We got the parts in time for the launch.

At the end and in the end

  • at the end (of) = at the final point of something. At the end of the meeting, we agreed the budget.
  • in the end = finally, after some time or discussion. In the end, we went with the second supplier.

In the team or on the team

British speakers usually say in the team: There are six people in my team. American speakers say on the team. You'll hear both in international companies. "At short notice" and "on holiday" are also British; Americans tend to say "on short notice" and "on vacation".

How to learn them

Prepositions are the clearest case where rules only take you so far. What works is collecting the phrases you actually need. When you read an email from a native speaker, notice the prepositions in the first two lines and copy the phrase, not just the word. "On behalf of", "at short notice", "in the attachment": each of these is one unit.

Then try a five-minute test once a week. Write three sentences about your week, using "in", "on" and "at" with times and work situations. Check them against this page. You'll quickly see which ones you are sure of and which ones you're guessing.
