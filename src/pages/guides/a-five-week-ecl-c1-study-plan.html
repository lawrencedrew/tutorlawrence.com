A five-week ECL C1 study plan

Five weeks won't take you from B2 to C1, but it can turn a borderline C1 into a pass. This plan uses about five hours a week, puts your weakest module first, and saves the official sample tests for when they are most useful.

Before you start: the rule that shapes the plan

Since August 2026 ECL is modular. You need at least 60% in each module, reading, writing, listening and speaking, to pass that module. Each passed module gets its own certificate, and if you pass all four within a year of your first exam you can request a complex certificate.

That changes how you prepare. One strong module can no longer carry a weak one, so your weakest module gets the most time. The good news is that a module you pass stays passed: if one goes wrong, you retake only that one.

Source: ECL, Passing ECL exams. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

What you'll need

  • Official practice material. ECL's C1 sample test booklet contains two complete tests with answer keys and audio. ECL also publishes a free C1 writing sample task and an officially assessed C1 writing sample.
  • The C1 topic list from the ECL website, for speaking and writing preparation.
  • A printed dictionary, monolingual or bilingual, the one you'll take into the writing module. Thematic dictionaries are not allowed.
  • Someone to speak with. The speaking test is in pairs, so you need a practice partner.

Sources: ECL C1 sample test booklet, C1 writing sample, topic list. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

Week 1: diagnose

  • Do Test 1 from the sample booklet, all four modules if possible, under real timings: reading 45 minutes, writing 75 minutes, listening as played. Speaking can be a recorded practice with your partner.
  • Mark it. Reading and listening: count correct items out of 20. You need 12 for 60%. Writing: check every bullet point is covered and properly developed.
  • Rank your four modules from weakest to strongest. Write the ranking down. The rest of the plan uses it.

Week 2: fix the method

Most early points come from method, not new language. This week, spend about two of your five hours on your weakest module and one hour on each of the others.

  • Reading: practise reading both sides of a gap and keeping short answers to a few words. See ECL C1 reading strategies.
  • Listening: practise a fixed routine for the first listening, the pause and the second listening.
  • Writing: write one task. Plan it in five minutes with one line per bullet point, then write it in 25.
  • Speaking: one 15-minute session with your partner on a C1 topic, focusing on asking each other questions.

Week 3: build the language

Now add range, because the C1 writing and speaking criteria reward vocabulary that is well chosen and relevant to the topic.

  • Pick five C1 topic areas, for example work, media, environment, public life and education.
  • For each, collect 10 useful phrases from real English texts and use each one in a sentence of your own. Collocations matter more than rare words: "tackle a problem", "a growing concern", "raise awareness".
  • Write two tasks this week, one formal (an email or report) and one opinion text (an essay or forum post).
  • Daily: 15 minutes of listening to English news or interviews.

Week 4: the second full test

  • Do Test 2 from the sample booklet under exam conditions. Compare each module with week 1.
  • Re-rank your modules. Usually the order changes. Focus the last week on whatever is now weakest, especially anything near the 60% line.
  • Have your writing checked against the five criteria, not just corrected. Knowing which criterion lost you points tells you what to fix. The criteria are explained in how ECL writing is marked.

Week 5: rehearse, don't cram

  • Two speaking mocks with a partner, full format: a short warm-up, a guided conversation on four bullet points, then a picture monologue each. Remember there is no preparation time in the real exam, so practise starting straight away.
  • One more timed writing session: two tasks in 75 minutes.
  • Revisit your reading and listening error logs and redo the items you got wrong.
  • Stop new material two days before. Check the practical rules: photo ID, black or blue pen, no correction fluid, your dictionary, and arriving early. Late arrivals aren't admitted once listening has started.

Sources: ECL exam regulations, candidate handbook and FAQ. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

A typical week for a busy professional

MondayReading task, timed, plus error log45 min

Every dayEnglish news or podcast on the commute15 min

If you miss a day, don't try to catch up by doubling the next one. Drop the Friday vocabulary session instead. The timed practice matters more.
