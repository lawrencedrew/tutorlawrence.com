How to go from band 6.5 to 7 in IELTS Writing

At 6.5 you're already writing well. Getting to 7 rarely means learning more English. It usually means more control: a clearer position, ideas you develop fully, and fewer small errors.

What actually changes between 6 and 7

A 6.5 often means some of your criteria are at 7 and some at 6. So the first job is to find which ones are at 6. Here are the key differences in the official band descriptors for Task 2.

CriterionBand 6Band 7
Task ResponseSome ideas "may be insufficiently developed"; conclusions "may be unclear, unjustified or repetitive""A clear and developed position"; main ideas "extended and supported"
Coherence and CohesionCohesion "may be faulty or mechanical"Cohesive devices, including reference and substitution, used "flexibly"
Lexical Resource"Rather restricted range" or "lack of precision""Some flexibility and precision"; some less common items
Grammatical Range and AccuracyComplex structures less accurate than simple ones"Error-free sentences are frequent"

The common thread is control. IELTS itself describes the step from 6 to 7 as a move from functional to confident writing.

Sources: the IELTS Writing band descriptors (PDF) and Differences between IELTS 6 and 7 for productive skills, both on ielts.org. Check them before your exam in case anything has changed.

Fix 1: develop fewer ideas, more deeply

This is the most common reason I see essays stuck at 6 for Task Response. The writer lists three or four reasons in a paragraph, each with one sentence. It looks thorough, but none of them is developed.

Band 6 style: "Working from home saves time. It also reduces costs. In addition, employees can concentrate better."

Band 7 style: "The clearest benefit of working from home is the time saved on commuting. An employee who previously spent an hour each way on the train gains ten hours a week, which can be used for rest, family or further study. Over a year, this can make a real difference to both well-being and productivity."

One idea, explained, with a concrete example and a consequence. Two body paragraphs built like this will do more than four thin ones.

Fix 2: make your position impossible to miss

Write your opinion in the introduction, keep every body paragraph pointing towards it, and repeat it in the conclusion in different words. If your conclusion says "there are advantages and disadvantages", the examiner has to guess what you think. That is exactly what "unclear" conclusions means in the band 6 descriptor.

Fix 3: replace mechanical linking with reference

Band 7 mentions reference and substitution. That means connecting sentences with words that point back, rather than with a linker at the start of every sentence.

Mechanical: "Many cities have traffic problems. Moreover, traffic problems cause pollution. Furthermore, pollution affects health."

Natural: "Many cities suffer from heavy traffic. This leads to high levels of pollution, which in turn affects residents' health."

"This" and "which" do the work, and the sentences flow. More examples in linking words that examiners actually reward.

Fix 4: precise words, not rare words

Students at 6.5 often try to reach 7 by adding impressive vocabulary. It frequently backfires, because a rare word used slightly wrongly signals a lack of precision.

  • "Big problem" → "serious problem" or "major concern"
  • "Do a crime" → "commit a crime"
  • "People" (used ten times) → "employees", "residents", "young adults", depending on who you mean
  • "Things like this" → "such measures", "these policies"

Collocation, the natural partnership between words, is where most of the gain is.

Fix 5: count your clean sentences

Band 7 needs error-free sentences to be frequent. Take your last essay and mark each sentence as clean or not clean. If fewer than half are clean, accuracy is your main problem, and adding more complex grammar will make it worse.

Most students have a personal list of five or six repeated errors: articles, plural -s, subject-verb agreement, comma splices, a few preposition habits. Find yours and check for those specifically in the last five minutes of the test.

A four-week plan

  1. Week 1: write two Task 2 essays and get them marked criterion by criterion. Identify your lowest criterion and your personal error list.

  2. Week 2: work only on the lowest criterion. If it's Task Response, practise planning and developing one idea per paragraph.

  3. Week 3: rewrite one of your old essays to band 7 standard, then write two new ones under timed conditions.

  4. Week 4: full timed tests, both tasks in 60 minutes, with checking against your error list.
