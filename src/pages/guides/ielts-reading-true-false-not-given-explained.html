IELTS Reading: True, False, Not Given explained

The hard part isn't True. It's telling False from Not Given. The rule is simple: False means the text says the opposite. Not Given means the text doesn't say either way.

The official definitions

The instructions in the test read:

  • TRUE if the statement agrees with the information
  • FALSE if the statement contradicts the information
  • NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this

There's a related question type, Yes / No / Not Given. It works the same way, but it tests the writer's views or claims rather than facts. "No" means the statement contradicts the writer's view, and "Not Given" means it neither agrees with nor contradicts it.

The Reading test is 60 minutes for 40 questions, and the British Council lists no extra time to transfer answers. So on paper, writing your answers on the answer sheet has to fit inside the 60 minutes.

Sources: the IELTS Academic Reading sample tasks (PDF), Academic test format in detail on ielts.org, and the British Council's Reading test format page. Check them before your exam in case anything has changed.

False versus Not Given, with an example

Text: "The bridge was completed in 1932, two years later than planned, after a dispute between the city council and the main contractor."

StatementAnswerWhy
The bridge opened later than originally intended.TRUE"Two years later than planned" means the same thing.
The bridge was finished on schedule.FALSEThe text says the opposite: it was late.
The dispute was about money.NOT GIVENThere was a dispute, but the text doesn't say what it was about.
The contractor was replaced after the dispute.NOT GIVENPlausible, but the text says nothing about it.

The test for False: can you point to words in the text that make the statement impossible? If yes, it's False. If you can only say "the text doesn't mention that", it's Not Given.

The traps that catch good readers

1. Using your own knowledge

If a statement says "Coffee contains caffeine" and the text never mentions caffeine, the answer is Not Given, even though it's true in real life. You're checking the text, not the world. Strong readers with good general knowledge fall into this one most.

2. Qualifying words

Small words change everything. Watch for: all, some, most, only, always, usually, never, the first, the largest.

Text: "Most of the participants were under thirty."
Statement: "All the participants were under thirty." → FALSE. "Most" contradicts "all".

Text: "The museum is the second largest in Europe."
Statement: "The museum is the largest in Europe." → FALSE. If it's second, it can't be first.

Compare: Text: "The museum is one of the largest in Europe." Statement: "The museum is the largest in Europe." → NOT GIVEN. It might be the largest, or it might not. The text doesn't say.

3. Matching words, different meaning

The statement often reuses words from the text but changes the relationship. "Sales fell after the price rise" in the text versus "The price rise was caused by falling sales" in the statement. Same words, reversed cause. That's False.

4. Partly true

If half the statement is True and the other half isn't mentioned, the whole statement is Not Given. Every part must match for True.

A method that works under time pressure

  1. Read the statement and underline the key idea, plus any qualifying words.

  2. Scan the text for the matching section. Look for names, numbers and synonyms, not identical phrases.

  3. Read that section carefully, a sentence before and after.

  4. Ask: does it say the same, the opposite, or nothing? Same is True. Opposite is False. Nothing is Not Given.

  5. Don't spend more than about 90 seconds on one statement. Make your best choice, mark it, and come back if you have time.

One thing I tell every student: if you can't find the information after a proper search, that is itself evidence for Not Given. Students sometimes keep searching for minutes because they're sure it must be there.

Writing the answer

Write the answer form the instructions ask for. If the question says TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN, don't write YES or NO, and the other way round. The British Council notes that incorrect spelling is marked wrong in Reading, so write the words carefully.

There's no negative marking, so never leave a box empty. If you run out of time, guess.

How to practise

For every practice answer, write one line explaining why: the words in the text that make it True or False, or "not mentioned" for Not Given. When you check, look at the reason, not just the answer. Most errors come from one of the four traps above, and once you know which trap you fall into, you can watch for it.
