ECL writing tasks: how they are marked and how to score higher

Each ECL writing task is marked by two assessors on five criteria, and one of them, covering the bullet points, can sink the whole task. Knowing what each criterion rewards tells you exactly where to spend your 75 minutes.

The writing module in brief

At C1 you have 75 minutes to write two texts of about 200 words each. Each task gives you a situation and four bullet points. The text can be a comment or forum post, an email, a message, a report, an article or an essay. A printed dictionary, monolingual or bilingual, is allowed in this module only. Thematic dictionaries are not.

At B2 it is 60 minutes and about 150 words per text, with the same criteria. Since August 2026 you need at least 60% in the writing module to pass it.

Source: ECL exam format page and Passing ECL exams. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

The five criteria

Two assessors mark each of your texts independently. Each criterion gets 0 to 5 points.

The rule that matters most

The official criteria say that if a task gets 0 for communicative effectiveness, the whole task scores 0, however good the grammar. That happens when none of the bullet points is developed or the content is irrelevant.

Below that, the descriptors are very concrete:

  • 5: all four bullet points covered and developed
  • 3: two covered properly, two only partly
  • 1: incomplete task, partly misunderstood, many irrelevant parts

So the cheapest points in the whole exam are these: write a real paragraph, not a single line, for every bullet point. In my marking, the most common reason for a weak task score isn't bad English. It's a fourth bullet point squeezed into one sentence because time ran out.

How to raise each score

Communicative effectiveness

Before you write, number the bullet points on the question paper and plan one sentence of content for each. ECL's own writing guide says you don't have to follow the bullet points in their original order. Group them in whatever way makes the text flow, as long as each one is clearly there.

Written accuracy

This includes paragraphing, which is easy to get right. Separate paragraphs clearly with a line space or an indent: ECL's writing guide specifically reminds candidates not to forget this. Write legibly too, because an assessor can't credit what they can't read.

Style

Match the register to the reader. A forum post can be personal and direct. A formal email or report shouldn't contain contractions such as "don't" or phrases like "a lot of". Show organisation with linking that does real work:

  • "That said," instead of a third "However,"
  • "This is partly because…" instead of "Because…" at the start of a sentence
  • "Taken together, these points suggest…" for a conclusion

Vocabulary

The C1 descriptors talk about vocabulary that is "well-chosen" and "relevant to the topic". That means precision, not rare words. Compare:

VaguePrecise
The internet has a big effect on people's relationships.Social media makes it easier to maintain weak ties but may weaken close friendships.
There are many problems with information online.Much of what circulates online is unverified, and algorithms reward content that provokes.
Companies should do more about data.Companies should be more transparent about how long they retain personal data.

Use the dictionary to check a collocation or spelling, not to hunt for impressive words. A word you met five minutes ago in a dictionary is the word most likely to be used wrongly.

Formal accuracy

Range counts as well as accuracy. Make sure each text has a few structures beyond simple sentences, for example:

  • a conditional: "Had I known…, I would…"
  • a passive where it is natural: "It is often assumed that…"
  • a relative clause: "…, which is why…"

Then check your known weak spots in the last five minutes: articles, prepositions, and subject-verb agreement after long subjects.

Word count

The task asks for about 200 words at C1. The criteria don't set a penalty for length, but ECL's B2 writing guide advises aiming for the required number and staying within about 10% over it. That fits what I'd advise anyway: a text far too short can't develop four bullet points, and a text far too long eats the time you need for the second task.

A timing plan for 75 minutes

  1. 5 minutes: plan task 1, one line per bullet point.

  2. 25 minutes: write task 1.

  3. 5 minutes: plan task 2.

  4. 25 minutes: write task 2.

  5. 15 minutes: check both and copy anything still in draft onto the answer sheet. Only what is on the answer sheet gets marked.
