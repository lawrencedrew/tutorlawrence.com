Linking words for IELTS that examiners actually reward

More linking words won't raise your score. Using them accurately, and connecting ideas in other ways too, will. Here is what the band descriptors actually ask for, and how to give it to them.

What the descriptors say

In Writing, linking is part of Coherence and Cohesion. The band 7 descriptor mentions "a range of cohesive devices including reference and substitution" used "flexibly but with some inaccuracies". At band 6, cohesion "may be faulty or mechanical".

In Speaking, it's part of Fluency and Coherence. Band 7 describes "flexible use of spoken discourse markers, connectives and cohesive features". At band 6, they're used "though not always appropriately".

So the key words are flexible and appropriate. Not "many". And "reference and substitution" tells you that linking isn't only about words like "however".

Sources: the IELTS Writing band descriptors and Speaking band descriptors (PDFs) on ielts.org. Check them before your exam in case anything has changed.

What "mechanical" looks like

This is the paragraph I see most often from students who have learned a list of linkers:

Before: "Firstly, technology has changed education. Moreover, students can study online. Furthermore, online courses are cheap. In addition, they are flexible. Therefore, more people study online."

Every sentence starts with a linker, and several of them mean the same thing. It reads like a list rather than an argument.

After: "Technology has transformed education, most obviously through online learning. Courses of this kind are usually cheaper than traditional ones and can be fitted around work, which explains why so many adults now choose them."

Fewer linkers, but the connections are clearer: "this kind", "traditional ones" and "which" all link back to earlier ideas.

Reference and substitution, in plain English

These are the cohesive devices most students underuse, and they're named in the band 7 descriptor.

  • Reference: pointing back with "this", "that", "these", "it", "they", "such". "Governments could tax sugary drinks. This would reduce consumption."
  • Summary nouns: "this problem", "this approach", "such measures", "these changes". They connect and add precision at the same time.
  • Substitution: replacing a repeated word with "one", "ones" or "do so". "Some cities have built cycle lanes. Others have yet to do so."
  • Relative clauses: "…which means that…", "…, which is why…"

A quick check: if you have "this" on its own at the start of a sentence, try adding a noun. "This is a problem" becomes "This shortage is a problem". It's clearer, and it shows vocabulary.

Linkers that often cause errors

  • "On the contrary" doesn't mean "on the other hand". It rejects what was just said: "The plan wasn't cheap. On the contrary, it was very expensive."
  • "Besides" means "in addition to", not "except", and at the start of a sentence it sounds conversational. In essays, "in addition" is safer.
  • "Although… but" is a double link. Use one: "Although it is expensive, it is popular."
  • "In conclusion" mid-essay, or "to sum up" with new ideas after it. Save conclusion signals for the last paragraph.
  • "Last but not least" sounds like a speech at a wedding. Use "Finally".

Linking in Speaking

Spoken English uses different connectors from essays. "Moreover" in a Speaking answer sounds like you're reading. Natural spoken discourse markers include:

  • "Actually…", "To be honest…", "I mean…"
  • "The thing is…", "The main reason is…"
  • "Having said that…", "Then again…"
  • "So basically…", "Anyway…" (to return to the point)

Example: "I love living in the city. Having said that, the noise can be a problem, especially in summer. The thing is, everyone has their windows open."

A five-minute check for your next essay

  1. Highlight every linker at the start of a sentence.

  2. Delete any that repeat a meaning already given, and read the sentence again. Often it's better without.

  3. Replace one or two with reference: "this approach", "such policies".

  4. Check each remaining linker means what you think, using the table above.
