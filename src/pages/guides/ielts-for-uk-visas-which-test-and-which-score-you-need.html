IELTS for UK visas: which test and which score you need

For most UK visas a normal IELTS certificate is not enough: you need IELTS for UKVI, taken at a Home Office-approved centre, with a minimum score in every skill. Here is how to work out which version and which score apply to you.

I'm an English teacher, not an immigration adviser. Everything below comes from the official GOV.UK and IELTS pages linked under each section, and the rules do change. Read those pages yourself before you book, and if your situation is unusual, ask the people handling your application.

Step one: find the CEFR level for your visa

The Home Office doesn't set an IELTS score. It sets a level on the CEFR scale (A1 up to C2), and then accepts certain tests as proof of that level. So start with your visa route, not with IELTS.

Visa routeLevel required
Skilled WorkerB2 (B1 if you are extending a Skilled Worker visa you held before 8 January 2026)
Student, bachelor's degree or aboveB2
Student, below degree levelB1
High Potential Individual, Scale-up, Innovator FounderB2 (Scale-up has the same B1 exception for existing visa holders)
Family visaA1 for a first application, A2 to extend
Settlement (indefinite leave to remain) and citizenshipB1 or above

The Skilled Worker change is the one catching people out this year. Older articles and forum posts still say B1. For new applications, GOV.UK now says B2.

Source: GOV.UK, English language requirement levels for immigration applications, Skilled Worker visa: knowledge of English and Prove your knowledge of English for citizenship and settling. Check them before your exam in case anything has changed.

Step two: choose the right IELTS for UKVI test

There are three IELTS tests on the approved list for UK visas:

  • IELTS for UKVI Academic: for undergraduate or postgraduate study, or professional registration.
  • IELTS for UKVI General Training: for work, training or study below degree level.
  • IELTS for UKVI Life Skills: at A1, A2 or B1, for family visas, indefinite leave to remain and citizenship. It tests only listening and speaking, and the A2 test is only available in the UK.

The UKVI Academic and General Training tests have the same design, format and difficulty as the standard tests. What changes is where and how you take them: they must be taken at a Home Office-approved test centre, with extra security checks. In practice, that means preparing for IELTS for UKVI is exactly the same as preparing for normal IELTS. The difference is all in the booking.

If you are not sure whether you need Academic or General Training, my guide to IELTS Academic vs General Training explains how the two differ.

Source: British Council, IELTS for UKVI tests for UK visas. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

Step three: the score that matches your level

For IELTS for UKVI Academic or General Training, the score applies to each of the four skills, not to your overall band:

CEFR levelWhat you need
A1A pass in IELTS for UKVI Life Skills A1
A2A pass in IELTS for UKVI Life Skills A2 (UK only)
B1A pass in Life Skills B1, or at least 4.0 in each of the four skills
B2At least 5.5 in each of the four skills
C1At least 7.0 in each of the four skills

Source: British Council, IELTS for UKVI tests for UK visas, and the GOV.UK list of approved secure English language tests. Check them before your exam in case anything has changed.

The "each skill" rule is where people fail

This is the point I spend most time on with visa candidates. A Skilled Worker applicant with 7.0 in listening, 6.5 in reading, 6.0 in speaking and 5.0 in writing has an overall band of 6.0, which sounds comfortable for B2. It isn't. The 5.0 in writing is below 5.5, so the result doesn't meet the requirement.

So don't aim for an overall score. Find your weakest skill and make sure it is safely above the line, with a bit of margin for a bad day. For most people I teach, that weak skill is writing, because it is the one they use least at work. If that sounds like you, start with the writing mistakes I mark every week.

Students: check with your university first

If you are going to study at degree level or above, GOV.UK says your university can assess your English itself, and it may ask for a different test. It still has to be equivalent to B2. Many universities also set their own, higher score. Ask the admissions team which test and score they want before you book anything. You only need a SELT if you have no other qualification that proves your English.

Source: GOV.UK, Student visa: knowledge of English. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

Four practical checks before you pay

  1. Read your route's GOV.UK page today, not a blog post from last year. Note the level and any exceptions.

  2. Book the test with "UKVI" in its name at an approved centre. A standard IELTS result taken at the same building is a different product.

  3. Check how long your result stays valid. GOV.UK warns that some test results only last two years, so plan the test date around your application date.

  4. Check retake options with the test provider. IELTS says the UK government has approved One Skill Retake for IELTS for UKVI, but it isn't offered at every centre and has its own conditions, so confirm it with your test centre rather than assuming.

If anything above doesn't match what you read on GOV.UK, trust GOV.UK.
