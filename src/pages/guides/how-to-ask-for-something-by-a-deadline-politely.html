How to ask for something by a deadline, politely

The polite way to give a deadline in English is a question, a specific day and a short reason. "Could you send it by Thursday, as I'm presenting on Friday?" is both friendly and hard to ignore.

The three-part formula

  1. A question, not an order. "Could you…?" / "Would you be able to…?"

  2. A specific deadline. A day, and a time if it matters: "by 12pm on Thursday".

  3. A reason. "…as the client meeting is on Friday morning."

The reason is the part people leave out, and it's the part that makes the difference. A deadline without a reason feels like pressure. A deadline with a reason feels like teamwork. The reader understands why the date matters and is much more likely to respect it.

Vague versus clear

Vague or pushyClear and polite
Please send it ASAP.Could you send it by the end of Wednesday?
I need this urgently.Would you be able to send this today? The order closes at 5pm.
Send me the figures by Friday.Could you send me the figures by Friday? I need them for the monthly report.
When you have time, could you check this?Could you check this by Tuesday, if possible?
Waiting for your feedback.Could you send me any feedback by 10 June?

Notice that "when you have time" is in the wrong column. It sounds polite, but it's a trap: it tells the reader the task isn't important. If you have a deadline, say it.

Be precise about the date

Deadlines cause misunderstandings when they're vague. A few common problems:

  • "By Friday" can mean Friday morning, Friday 5pm, or Friday midnight. If it matters, add the time: "by 3pm on Friday".
  • "End of the week" means Friday for most offices, but not all. Name the day.
  • "Next Friday" is ambiguous even for native speakers. Some mean this coming Friday, others mean the Friday after. Write "Friday 14 March".
  • Time zones. Working across countries? "By 5pm UK time" saves an argument.
  • "Until" and "by". "Send it by Friday" means at any time up to Friday. "Send it until Friday" is a common error and doesn't make sense in English.

That last one is the mistake I correct most in deadline emails, especially from German, Polish and Hungarian speakers, because the same word covers both meanings in those languages.

Softer and firmer versions

You can adjust the tone depending on who you're writing to and how important the deadline is.

Softer (a senior person, a client, a favour):

  • Would you be able to send it by Thursday, if possible?
  • I'd be grateful if you could let me have it by the 20th.
  • Would Thursday be realistic for you?

Neutral (everyday work):

  • Could you send it by Thursday?
  • Can you let me know by the end of today?

Firmer (a deadline that cannot move):

  • I'll need it by Thursday at the latest.
  • The deadline is fixed, so I'll need it by 12pm on Thursday.
  • If I don't have it by Thursday, we won't be able to include it in the bid.

The last example is firm but still polite, because it explains a consequence instead of making a threat.

Ask them to confirm

One extra line turns a hopeful request into an agreement: "Does that work for you?" or "Could you let me know if that's a problem?" Now, if the date is unrealistic, you find out today, not on Thursday afternoon.

Full examples

To a colleague:

"Hi Lukas, could you send me your section of the report by 12pm on Wednesday? I need to put it all together for Thursday's board meeting. Does that work for you? Thanks, Clara"

To a client:

"Dear Mr Oliveira, to keep the project on schedule, would you be able to send us the signed contract by Friday 7 March? Our design team is ready to start on the 10th. Please let me know if you need more time. Kind regards, Clara"

To a supplier, with a firmer tone:

"Hi Tom, we'll need the revised quote by 5pm UK time on Tuesday at the latest. Our budget is approved on Wednesday morning, and we can't include late quotes. Thanks for your help, Clara"

Put the deadline where people will see it

Readers skim. If the deadline is hidden in the middle of a long paragraph, it will be missed, and then it's your problem. Two habits help:

  • Put the deadline in the subject line: "Contract signature needed by Fri 7 March".
  • Give the request its own short paragraph, with nothing else in it.
