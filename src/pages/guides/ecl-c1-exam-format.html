ECL C1 English exam: format, timing and scoring explained

The ECL C1 exam tests four skills as separate modules, needs 60% in each one, and has no grammar section. Here is exactly what happens on the day, and what that means for how you prepare.

The exam at a glance

Since August 2026, the ECL exam works as four separate modules: reading, writing, listening and speaking. Each one is passed or failed on its own. If you pass all four within one year, counted from the date of your first successful module, you can ask for a combined certificate.

What you need to pass

At least 60% in every module, which is 15 of the 25 points for each skill. This rule applies from August 2026. Before then, a weak skill could be balanced by stronger ones, as long as it reached 40% and the overall average reached 60%. That safety net has gone.

In practice, this means your weakest skill decides whether you pass. A brilliant speaker who scores 14 out of 25 in reading fails that module. Find your weakest skill early and bring it up to safe ground before you polish the others.

Reading: 45 minutes, 20 questions

Two texts such as a report, a blog post, a brochure or a newspaper article. Questions are multiple choice, matching, gap-filling and short answers. Each correct answer is worth 1.25 points, and an answer with anything extra or wrong in it scores nothing.

Tip: for short answers, write only what the question asks for. Copying a whole sentence "to be safe" can cost you the point.

Writing: 75 minutes, two texts

You write two texts of about 200 words each. Each one is built around four bullet points, and the type can be a comment or forum post, a report, an article, an email or an essay. A printed dictionary, monolingual or bilingual, is allowed in this part only.

Two examiners mark your writing separately, each giving up to 5 points for five criteria, including how well you complete the task, your range of vocabulary and whether your style fits the reader.

Tip: cover all four bullet points, clearly and in order. Missing one is the easiest way to lose marks for task completion, and it happens most when people run short of time.

Listening: about 30–35 minutes, 20 questions

Two recordings, such as a conversation, an interview, a lecture or a news report, with multiple-choice and short-answer questions. You hear each recording twice, with time to read the questions before and after.

Tip: use the first listening to get the overall meaning and the second to check details. Don't spend the gap between them worrying about one missed answer.

Speaking: about 18–20 minutes in pairs

After a short warm-up that isn't marked, you and your partner have a guided conversation on four bullet points. Then each of you gives a short talk based on a set of four pictures. Two examiners score you on five criteria.

Tip: in the paired task, you are marked on interaction as well as language. Ask your partner what they think, build on their points and disagree politely. Talking at each other in two monologues scores lower.

How to prepare in practice

  1. Take a full practice test early, timed, to find your weakest skill. The ECL centre publishes sample papers.

  2. Write to the clock. 75 minutes for two texts is tight. Practise planning each text in five minutes.

  3. Get your writing marked against the criteria, not just corrected. Knowing which criterion lost you the points tells you what to fix.

  4. Practise the paired speaking task with another person, not alone. Interaction is a skill in itself.
