Common mistakes Hungarian speakers make in English

Hungarian and English work in very different ways, so a few mistakes come up again and again, even at C1. The good news is that they are predictable, and once you know where they come from they are easier to fix.

Grammar that Hungarian doesn't have

He and she

Hungarian has one word, ő, for both "he" and "she", and the possessive ending is the same for "his" and "her". So even very fluent speakers mix them up when they talk quickly: "I spoke to Anna and he said…"

This one matters more than it seems. If you say "he" about a female colleague, your listener may think you're talking about someone else. In a story with two people, it can make the whole thing confusing.

Fix: slow down on the pronoun after a name. When you mention a woman, consciously say "she" in the next sentence. If you catch the mistake, correct it straight away: "…he, sorry, she said…". Native speakers won't notice the correction, but they will notice the mistake.

Plurals after numbers

In Hungarian, a noun after a number stays singular: három könyv, öt év. In English, it must be plural.

What people sayCorrect English
I have three year of experience.I have three years of experience.
We have two new client.We have two new clients.
Many people was there.Many people were there.

One exception confuses things further: when a number and noun work as an adjective before another noun, English uses the singular with a hyphen. A three-year contract, a five-minute break, a two-day course.

One past tense, three in English

Modern Hungarian has one past tense. English splits the past into past simple, present perfect and past continuous, and the choice changes the meaning. The most common error is using the present for something that started in the past and continues:

  • "2020 óta itt dolgozom" becomes "I work here since 2020". Correct: I've worked here since 2020.
  • "Két éve vagyok ebben a pozícióban" becomes "I am in this role for two years". Correct: I've been in this role for two years.

See since or for and when British speakers use the present perfect for the full picture.

Questions without "do"

Hungarian turns a statement into a question with intonation alone. English normally needs an auxiliary verb.

What people sayCorrect English
You finished the report?Have you finished the report?
Where you work?Where do you work?
Why he didn't come?Why didn't he come?

"You finished the report?" with rising intonation is possible in casual English, to check something you already expect. But in a meeting or an interview it sounds unfinished. Use the full question.

Double negatives

Hungarian uses several negative words together: Nem láttam senkit. In standard English, one negative is enough. "I didn't see nobody" sounds uneducated. Say I didn't see anybody or I saw nobody. The same with "I don't know nothing" (I don't know anything).

"The" with general ideas

Hungarian uses the definite article when talking about things in general: Szeretem a zenét. Az élet szép. English doesn't, for uncountable and plural nouns used generally.

  • "The life is hard" → Life is hard.
  • "The customers want lower prices" (meaning all customers in general) → Customers want lower prices.
  • "I'm interested in the marketing" → I'm interested in marketing.

Use "the" when you mean specific ones: The customers we met on Monday want lower prices.

Vocabulary traps

  • lehetőség is both "possibility" and "opportunity". In English they are different. A job offer is an opportunity, not "a possibility". "Thank you for the possibility" should be Thank you for the opportunity.
  • tanul covers both "learn" and "study". You study for an exam or at university, and you learn a skill or a fact. "I learn at university" should be I study at university.
  • aktuális means current or relevant now. "The actual situation" in English means "the real situation". Say the current situation.
  • márka is a brand. A "mark" in English is a grade, a sign or a stain.
  • szimpatikus means likeable or nice. "Sympathetic" means understanding about someone's problem.

Pronunciation

First-syllable stress

This is the most useful thing I can tell a Hungarian speaker about pronunciation. Hungarian always stresses the first syllable. English doesn't, and stress on the wrong syllable can make a word hard to recognise, even if every sound is perfect.

Watch these, where the stress is not on the first syllable: deVELop, aNALysis, eCONomy, comPUter, hoTEL, caREER, engiNEER. Plenty of words do start with the stress (MANagement, COMpany), so check new words in a dictionary that shows stress. For more, see word stress.

W and V

Hungarian has no /w/ sound, so "west" and "vest", "wine" and "vine" can sound the same. For /w/, round your lips as if you were going to say "oo", and don't let your top teeth touch your lip. Practise with "We were very worried."

Th

"Think" often becomes "tink" or "sink". Put the tip of your tongue lightly between your teeth and blow. It feels exaggerated at first, but it is the only way to get it right.

Where to start

Don't work on everything at once. In my experience, the two that make the biggest difference to how you come across at work are he/she and word stress, because they affect whether people understand you. Record yourself telling a two-minute story about a colleague, then listen for those two only.
