How to describe your job and responsibilities in English

Say what you do, who you do it for, and one result, in plain verbs. Most people describe their job title and stop. Here's how to describe the work itself, in interviews and anywhere else.

Job titles don't travel well

"I'm a specialist" or "I'm a coordinator" tells a British listener almost nothing. Job titles vary hugely between countries and companies, and some translate badly. What people understand is what you actually do all day.

So instead of "I'm a Senior Specialist in the Controlling Department", try "I work in finance. I put together the monthly reports that show the board where we're over or under budget."

A simple structure

  1. Field and company: "I work in logistics for a mid-sized manufacturer."

  2. Main responsibility: "I'm in charge of planning deliveries to our customers in Germany and Austria."

  3. Day-to-day tasks: "So a typical day involves checking stock, booking transport and dealing with any delays."

  4. One result or detail: "Last year I moved us to a new carrier, which cut our transport costs noticeably."

In a networking chat, stop after step 2. In an interview, use all four.

Key phrases

PurposePhrases
FieldI work in sales / IT / HR. / I'm in finance.
CompanyI work for a software company. / I work at a design agency in Lisbon.
ResponsibilityI'm responsible for… / I'm in charge of… / I look after… / I deal with…
Daily tasksMy day usually involves… / A big part of my job is… / I spend most of my time…
PeopleI manage a team of six. / I report to the finance director. / I work closely with…
Current projectAt the moment I'm working on… / This year I'm focusing on…

"I look after" is very British and very useful. "I look after our key accounts in Spain" sounds relaxed and natural, more so than "I am responsible for the management of".

The prepositions people mix up

This is where most mistakes happen, even at advanced level:

AvoidSay instead
I am responsible of the budget.I'm responsible for the budget.
I'm in charge for the team.I'm in charge of the team.
I work as analyst in a bank.I work as an analyst at a bank.
I take care about clients.I look after clients. / I take care of clients.
I'm dealing about complaints.I deal with complaints.
I'm working here since 2021.I've been working here since 2021.

"Work for" focuses on the employer, "work at" on the place or company name, and "work in" on the field or department. All three are common. See prepositions in business English for more.

Present simple or present continuous?

Use the present simple for your regular responsibilities: "I manage the supplier contracts." Use the present continuous for current, temporary projects: "At the moment I'm leading the move to a new ERP system."

A common mistake is using the continuous for everything: "I am managing the contracts, I am preparing reports…" It makes your permanent job sound temporary.

Make it concrete

Vague descriptions are the biggest weakness I hear. Compare:

Vague: "I'm responsible for different tasks connected with customers and also some administrative things."

Concrete: "I handle around thirty customer accounts, mainly in retail. I answer their questions about orders, sort out problems with deliveries, and prepare a quarterly review for each of the bigger ones."

The second version uses specific verbs (handle, answer, sort out, prepare), a number, and a type of customer. The listener can picture the job. For stronger verbs, see your CV in English: words that sound stronger. Most of them work in speech too.

Explaining internal jargon

Every company has its own words: internal system names, project codes, team nicknames. In an interview with a new employer, they mean nothing. Translate them into general terms:

  • Instead of "I update the SAP-MM module", say "I manage purchase orders in our ERP system."
  • Instead of "I'm on the Phoenix project", say "I'm on a project to move our customer data to a new platform."

If the name matters, add the explanation: "I work on Phoenix, which is our project to…"

A complete example

"I work in procurement for a construction company. I'm responsible for buying electrical materials for around twenty sites, so a big part of my job is getting quotes from suppliers, comparing prices and making sure deliveries arrive when the sites need them. I report to the head of purchasing and work closely with the project managers. At the moment I'm setting up a preferred supplier list, which should make ordering much quicker."

That's under a minute, and it would work as the "present" part of your answer to 'Tell me about yourself'.
