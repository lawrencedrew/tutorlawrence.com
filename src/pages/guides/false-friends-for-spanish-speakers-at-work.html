False friends for Spanish speakers at work

Spanish and English share thousands of words, which is a huge advantage. A few of them, though, look the same and mean something different. These are the ones that cause real confusion at work.

Why false friends are risky

A grammar mistake usually sounds like a mistake, so your listener corrects it in their head. A false friend sounds like perfect English with a different meaning. Your colleague doesn't know anything has gone wrong. They just understand something you didn't say.

The four I hear most in meetings

Actualmente and actually

This is the big one. "Actually, we work with three suppliers" doesn't mean "currently". To a British ear it means "in fact, contrary to what you think", as if you're correcting someone. British speakers use "actually" all the time to disagree politely: "Actually, I think the deadline is Friday." So if you use it to mean "now", people may hear a correction that isn't there.

Say currently, at the moment or right now: At the moment, we work with three suppliers. The same goes for "the actual price", which should be the current price.

Eventualmente and eventually

"Eventually" means something will definitely happen, but after a long time or a lot of effort: We eventually signed the contract after six months. If you write "Eventually, we can extend the deadline" meaning "if necessary", your reader understands that the deadline will be extended, sooner or later. Use if necessary, if needed or possibly.

Related: a contrato eventual is a temporary or fixed-term contract.

Discutir and discuss

In English, you can discuss something with a client for an hour and both leave happy. If you say "We discussed with the client" meaning there was a disagreement, nobody will realise there was a problem. For conflict, say we argued, or more diplomatically, we disagreed about or we had a difficult conversation about. Also, "discuss" takes no preposition: we discussed the price, not "discussed about".

Compromiso and compromise

"Our compromise with quality" sounds like you've lowered your standards to reach a deal. What you mean is our commitment to quality. "I have a compromise on Thursday" should be I've got a commitment, or more naturally, I've got something on or I'm busy on Thursday.

Words that need a different verb

Realizar rarely translates as "realise". Choose the verb that fits the noun:

  • realizar una reunión → hold a meeting
  • realizar un estudio → carry out a study
  • realizar un pago → make a payment
  • realizar una presentación → give a presentation

Introducir works for "introduce a new policy", but not for data. You enter a password, insert a card, and introduce a person.

Asistir and atender are confusing because they swap. Asistí a la reunión is "I attended the meeting". Atender a un cliente is "deal with" or "look after a customer". "I'll assist the meeting" sounds like you are helping to run it.

The embarrassing ones

These rarely come up in a report, but they do come up over lunch or at a team event.

  • embarazada means pregnant. "Embarrassed" is avergonzado/a.
  • constipado means you have a cold. "Constipated" in English is about your digestion, so say "I've got a cold".
  • molestar means bother or disturb. "Molest" in English means to sexually assault or abuse. Never use it for "Sorry to bother you". Say "Sorry to bother you" or "Sorry to interrupt".
  • sensible means sensitive. "Sensible" in English means practical and reasonable, so "sensitive data" is right and "sensible data" is not.

How to stop making them

Don't try to learn all of these. Pick the three you are most likely to use at work, probably actualmente, eventualmente and realizar, and search your sent emails for "actually", "eventually" and "realise". You will quickly see whether you have the habit.

Then build a replacement sentence about your own job: "At the moment, I'm responsible for the Iberian market." Say it aloud a few times until it comes out first.
