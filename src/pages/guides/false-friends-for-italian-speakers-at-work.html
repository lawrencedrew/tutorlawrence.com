False friends for Italian speakers at work

Italian gives you a head start with thousands of English words. A handful, though, look identical and mean something else. These are the ones that cause real misunderstandings in meetings and emails.

Why these matter more than grammar mistakes

When you make a grammar mistake, your listener usually notices and works out what you meant. A false friend is different. It sounds like perfect English, so your colleague simply understands the wrong thing. "We need a consistent increase in budget" is a correct sentence. It just doesn't mean what an Italian speaker usually intends.

The ones I correct most often

Attualmente and actually

"Actually, I manage the export team" doesn't mean "at the moment". To a British listener it means "in fact, despite what you think", as if you are correcting them. British speakers use "actually" constantly for polite disagreement: "Actually, I think that was last quarter." So use currently or at the moment instead.

Eventualmente and eventually

"Eventually" means "in the end, after a long time": We eventually found a new supplier. If you write "Eventually, we can postpone the call", the reader understands the call will be postponed at some point. What you mean is if necessary or if needed: If necessary, we can postpone the call.

Consistente and consistent

"A consistent increase" in English means an increase that happens steadily, at the same rate. "Un aumento consistente" means a large one. Say a substantial increase, a significant increase or simply a big increase.

Argomento and argument

"What's the argument of the meeting?" sounds like you expect a fight. Ask "What's the meeting about?" or "What's the topic?". In a presentation, "The next topic is…" or "Moving on to…" works well.

Controllare and control

"Can you control the figures?" sounds strange. You check figures, check an email before sending it, and double-check a booking. "Control" is about power or limits: control costs, quality control, a control panel.

Conveniente and convenient

"This supplier is more convenient" means they are easier to work with, perhaps closer or quicker. If you mean their prices are lower, say cheaper, better value or more competitive. "Is Tuesday convenient for you?" is correct and very useful: it means "Does Tuesday suit you?"

Stage and internship

English doesn't use "stage" for a work placement. Say internship. In British English, you'll also hear placement (especially as part of a degree) and work experience (usually short, for school students). On a CV: Six-month internship in the marketing department.

Licenziare and license

"He was licensed" means he received official permission, for example to practise as a lawyer. If someone lost their job, say he was dismissed, he was let go (softer) or he was made redundant (the role was cut, not his fault). A small British detail: the verb is spelt license and the noun licence.

Pretendere and pretend

"The client pretends a reply by Friday" means the client is acting as if they want one. Say the client expects or, if they're insisting, the client is demanding a reply by Friday.

The ones outside the office

These come up at lunch or at a team event, and some are embarrassing.

  • educato = polite, well-mannered. "Educated" means you've studied.
  • simpatico = nice, friendly, likeable. "Sympathetic" means understanding about someone's problem.
  • annoiato = bored. "Annoyed" means irritated.
  • sensibile = sensitive. "Sensible" means practical and reasonable.
  • parenti = relatives. "Parents" are only your mother and father.
  • preservativo = condom. A "preservative" is a chemical that keeps food fresh, conservante.

How to fix them

Search your sent emails for "actually", "eventually" and "consistent". Those three are the most common and the most likely to mislead a reader. If they appear often, you have found your first job. Write one correct sentence about your own work for each, and use it in your next meeting.
