IELTS Academic vs General Training: which one do you need?

The organisation you are applying to decides, not you. Academic is for university study and professional registration; General Training is mainly for work, migration and study below degree level. Listening and Speaking are identical, so the real difference is in Reading and Writing.

The short answer

  • IELTS Academic: for undergraduate or postgraduate study at a university that teaches in English, and for professional registration.
  • IELTS General Training: widely accepted for migration and residency, and for work and study below degree level.

The official advice is to check the government's or institution's website before you book. Don't guess from a friend's experience, because requirements differ between countries, universities and professional bodies. If you are applying to a UK visa, there's an extra step: you probably need the UKVI version of the test. My guide to IELTS for UK visas covers that.

Source: British Council, Which IELTS test should you take?. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

What is the same

SkillBoth tests
ListeningAbout 30 minutes, four parts, 40 questions
Speaking11–14 minutes with an examiner, three parts
ScoringA band from 0 to 9 for each skill, averaged for an overall band

So if your weak skill is speaking or listening, the choice of test changes nothing about how you prepare for it.

What is different

AcademicGeneral Training
Reading (60 min, 40 questions)Three long passages from academic-style textsThree sections: short everyday texts such as notices and adverts, then workplace texts, then one longer general-interest text
Writing Task 1 (150+ words)Describe visual information such as a graph, table or diagramA letter, which may be personal, semi-formal or formal
Writing Task 2 (250+ words)An essayAn essay

In both tests, Writing lasts 60 minutes and Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1.

Sources: IELTS, Academic test format in detail, General Training Reading General Training Writing and the IELTS scores guide. Check them before your exam in case anything has changed.

"Is General Training easier?"

I get asked this every month. My honest answer: it's different, not easy. Here is what I see in lessons.

Reading. The first General Training texts are short and familiar, which feels easier, but the final section is a long, more complex text, and there are still 40 questions in 60 minutes. Students who are used to reading reports at work often do fine with Academic passages too. The question types, such as True, False, Not Given, are the same kind in both, and they are where most marks are lost.

Writing Task 1. This is where people really feel the difference. Professionals who write reports at work often find the Academic graph task quite natural: pick out the main trend, compare, give figures. Others find the General Training letter easier to start but harder to get the tone right. The marking checks whether your tone fits the reader, and a letter to a friend written like a complaint to a bank loses marks.

Compare these two openings for the same situation, a friend who lent you a book:

  • Too formal for a friend: "Dear Anna, I am writing to inform you that I am in possession of your book."
  • Right for a friend: "Hi Anna, I've just realised I still have your book. Sorry it's taken me so long."

My guide to formal, semi-formal and informal letters goes through all three tones.

Task 2. Both tests have an essay, and this is the bigger half of your writing score either way. The skills are the same, and so is a structure that works for any question.

Common mix-ups to avoid

  • Booking Academic "because it looks better". If the visa or employer asks for General Training, an Academic result might not be accepted, and the reverse is true for universities. Check first.
  • Booking the standard test when you need UKVI. For many UK visas, the test must be the UKVI version at an approved centre. The content is the same, but the result is a different product.
  • Preparing with the wrong materials. Listening and Speaking practice is shared, but Reading and Writing Task 1 practice must match your version. Check the cover of any practice book.
  • Assuming the target score is the same. An organisation may set an overall score, a minimum in each skill, or both. Write down exactly what they ask for.

How to decide in five minutes

  1. Open the official page of the university, employer, professional body or visa route you are applying to.

  2. Find the exact test name. "IELTS Academic", "IELTS General Training", "IELTS for UKVI" or "any IELTS" all mean different things.

  3. Note the score rule: overall, per skill, or both.

  4. If two applications need different tests, for example a degree course and a work visa, ask both organisations whether one test can cover both before you pay twice.

  5. If the page is unclear, email them. A one-line answer from admissions is worth more than any forum thread.
