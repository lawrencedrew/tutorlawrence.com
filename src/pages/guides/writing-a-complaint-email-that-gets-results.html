Writing a complaint email that gets results

A complaint that gets results is calm, specific and ends with a clear request. Anger makes people defensive; facts and a deadline make them act.

Decide what you want before you write

Most complaint emails fail because they describe the problem in detail and then stop. The reader agrees it was bad, says sorry, and nothing happens. Before you write a word, finish this sentence: "I want them to…" Refund the payment? Send a replacement by Friday? Call me to discuss? That sentence becomes the most important line of your email.

The five-part structure

  1. Subject line with the reference. "Complaint: order 88214, damaged on delivery".

  2. One sentence of context. What you bought or arranged, and when.

  3. The facts of the problem. Dates, numbers, what was promised and what happened. No adjectives yet.

  4. The impact. Briefly, what it cost you: time, money, a client.

  5. Your request and a deadline. Exactly what you want, by when.

Facts beat feelings

EmotionalFactual
Your service is a disaster.This is the third late delivery since January.
Nobody ever answers the phone.I called on 4, 5 and 6 March and couldn't get through.
The quality is terrible.12 of the 50 units arrived with cracked screens. Photos attached.
This is unacceptable!!!This isn't the standard we agreed in our contract.
Do something immediately.Please send replacements by Friday 14 March.

The factual versions are harder to argue with. They also give the person who reads your email the evidence they need to fight for you inside their own company. Remember that the reader is often not the person who caused the problem.

Useful phrases

Stating the problem

  • I'm writing to complain about…
  • I'm writing about a problem with order 88214.
  • Unfortunately, the delivery we received on 3 March was incomplete.
  • According to our agreement, … However, …

Describing the impact

  • As a result, we had to postpone the launch.
  • This has caused serious problems for our production schedule.
  • We've had to cover the extra cost ourselves.

Making the request

  • Could you please arrange a full refund by 20 March?
  • I'd like you to replace the damaged items at no extra cost.
  • Please confirm by Friday how you plan to resolve this.
  • I'd appreciate a call to discuss how we can prevent this in future.

Escalating politely

  • If we can't resolve this by Friday, I'll need to raise it with our purchasing director.
  • We'd like to continue working with you, but we need to see this resolved.

A full example

"Subject: Complaint: order 88214, damaged items

Dear Mr Hughes,

I'm writing about order 88214, which we received on Monday 3 March.

Twelve of the 50 monitors arrived with cracked screens. I've attached photos of the damaged units and the delivery note, which our warehouse signed as "damaged packaging".

We had planned to install these at a client's office this week, so we've had to postpone the installation and explain the delay to the client.

Could you please send 12 replacement units by Friday 7 March, and arrange collection of the damaged ones? Please confirm by tomorrow whether this is possible.

We've been happy with your service until now, and I'd like to get this resolved quickly.

Kind regards,
Katarzyna Wójcik
Procurement Manager"

Notice the second-to-last line. A small positive note about the relationship makes the reader want to help you. It isn't weakness; it's strategy.

Tone: firm, not aggressive

  • No capital letters and no exclamation marks. They feel like shouting and make the reader defensive.
  • Avoid sarcasm. "Thanks for nothing" or "Great service, as always" can be misread, and they never help.
  • Use "we" for your company and "your" carefully. "Your mistake" sounds accusing. "The error on the invoice" is calmer and says the same thing.
  • Keep it to one screen. If the history is long, put it in an attachment or a numbered timeline.

If English feels too direct to you already, read how to sound less direct in English before you send. If it feels too soft, the opposite problem, check that your request has a specific date.

A tip from marking real complaint emails

The most common weakness I see is a request hidden in a question like "Is there anything you can do?" That hands the decision to the other side, and they'll choose the cheapest option. Name the outcome you want. You can still be polite: "Could you please send replacements by Friday?" is a question in form, but the content is a clear request.

Also, write the email when you're calm. If you're angry, draft it, save it, and reread it an hour later. You'll usually delete two sentences, and the email will be better without them.
