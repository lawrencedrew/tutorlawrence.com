ECL vs IELTS vs Cambridge: which English exam should you take?

Start with who needs to see your certificate, not with which exam sounds easiest. A UK visa, a Hungarian degree requirement and a CV line each point to a different exam.

The quick answer

  • You need English for a UK visa: take an approved Secure English Language Test such as IELTS for UKVI. Neither ECL nor Cambridge C1 Advanced is on the UK Home Office list.
  • You need a state-accredited language exam in Hungary, for example for a degree or a job: ECL English is state-accredited there from A2 to C1.
  • You are applying to universities or employers abroad: check which exams they list. Many name IELTS or Cambridge specifically, and a certificate they don't list may not count.
  • You want a certificate that shows a level and doesn't need renewing: ECL and Cambridge certificates don't expire, though the organisation reading them may set its own limits.

Sources: GOV.UK, approved secure English language tests; ECL, Recognition in Hungary. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

How the scoring differs, and why it matters

ECL is pass or fail at one level. You choose B2 or C1 in advance. Since August 2026 you need at least 60% in each module, and each module you pass gets its own certificate. If you pass all four within a year you can request a complex certificate. That makes ECL forgiving in one way: a bad listening day doesn't cancel your reading and writing.

IELTS gives a score, not a pass. Every candidate gets a band. That suits situations where the organisation sets the number, such as "7.0 overall, no band below 6.5".

Cambridge C1 Advanced is aimed at C1 but reports a scale score. 180–199 is certified at C1, 200–210 at C2, and 160–179 gets a B2 certificate, so a slightly weaker performance still leaves you with something.

Sources: ECL, Passing ECL exams; IELTS, How IELTS is scored; Cambridge English, C1 Advanced results factsheet. Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

How long the result lasts

  • ECL: certificates have no expiry date. Some organisations may ask for a recent result, for example that the first module is no older than a year.
  • Cambridge: the certificate is valid indefinitely, but each institution decides how old a result it will accept.
  • IELTS: the recommendation to institutions is not to accept results more than two years old unless you can show you have kept your English up.

Sources: ECL FAQ; Cambridge English, How long are my results valid for? Check it before your exam in case anything has changed.

Which one suits the way you work?

This is the part people forget. Beyond acceptance, each exam rewards slightly different strengths.

  • Strong at grammar exercises, nervous speaking to strangers? Cambridge has a Use of English section that rewards grammar and vocabulary knowledge directly. ECL and IELTS don't.
  • Comfortable in conversation? ECL and Cambridge both test speaking in pairs, so interaction with another candidate counts. IELTS speaking is just you and the examiner, which some people find calmer.
  • Worried about the writing? ECL allows a printed dictionary in its writing module. That doesn't make it easy, but it takes away the panic of one missing word.
  • Find long objective papers tiring? ECL Reading has 20 items, against 40 questions in IELTS Reading and 56 in Cambridge Reading and Use of English. That isn't easier at the same level, but each item carries more weight, so careless slips cost more.

Questions to ask before you book

  1. Who exactly will check my certificate? Get the requirement in writing, from the university page, the employer or the official guidance.

  2. Do they name an exam, or just a level? "B2 or above" may allow ECL. "IELTS 6.5" doesn't.

  3. Do they need a minimum in each skill? Some universities, employers or authorities set a minimum in every skill, so check the current requirement with the organisation.

  4. How recent must it be? This decides whether an older certificate still counts.
